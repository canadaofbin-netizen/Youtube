# Henri Tajfel (1971): Klee vs Kandinsky Paintings — How Meaningless Labels Spark Instant 'Us vs Them' Tribalism — Master Story (longform depth)

## 1. Cold open (the iconic moment)

During World War II, Polish-Jewish scientist **Henri Tajfel** survived five years inside prisoner-of-war camps only to walk out in 1945 and discover almost everyone he loved had been murdered in the Holocaust. Determined to understand how ordinary neighbors turn into deadly 'Us vs Them' enemies, Tajfel designed a **1971 Bristol schoolboy experiment** meant to be a boring **0% baseline**: split boys randomly by whether they preferred two abstract painters they'd never heard of (**Klee vs Kandinsky**), lock them in private cubicles with zero contact and zero self-profit, and slowly add conflict later (SRC1). Tajfel never got to Step 2 — because **within five minutes, 'Klee' boys cheerfully threw away 63% of their own teammate's money just to make sure 'Kandinsky' boys got even less**.

Recorded in 1971, Department of Psychology, University of Bristol, UK, the defining visual record centers on a 1971 Bristol psychology room with Klee and Kandinsky abstract prints beside schoolboys in blazers circling '7 vs 1' over '19 vs 25' on a large payoff matrix chart (SRC1, SRC_STILL). Searching for a zero-bias baseline at Bristol in 1971, Holocaust survivor Henri Tajfel discovered the Minimal Group Effect — assigning boys randomly to 'Klee' or 'Kandinsky' painting groups with zero contact or self-benefit caused them to sacrifice total group wealth (picking 7-vs-1 over 19-vs-25) simply so 'Us' came out ahead of 'Them' (SRC1).

## 2. World before (era, place, why it mattered)

Muzafer Sherif's Robbers Cave study (1954) assumed tribal prejudice required real competition over scarce prizes ('realistic conflict theory') — predicting that empty labels without competition would produce zero bias.

At Department of Psychology, University of Bristol, UK in 1971, **Henri Tajfel, Billig, Bundy & Flament's 1971 Bristol 'Minimal Group Paradigm' (Klee vs Kandinsky & Dot-Estimation Matrices)** turned an unresolved question about human mind and brain function into a controlled, reproducible empirical test (SRC1: Henri Tajfel, M. G. Billig, R. P. Bundy, Claude Flament (1971). Social categorization and intergroup behaviour. European Journal of Social Psychology).

## 3. People

- **Henri Tajfel**: Polish-Jewish Holocaust survivor and POW turned Bristol social psychologist who set out to find the MINIMUM baseline — expecting zero bias! — and accidentally discovered arbitrary labels alone ignite tribalism (SRC1).
- **64 Bristol Comprehensive-School Boys (ages 14–15)**: Classmates who knew each other well, sat in separate cubicles never knowing WHO was in which group, and allocated real money points via Tajfel's payoff matrices (SRC1).
- **John C. Turner & Toshio Yamagishi**: Tajfel's protégé who co-built Social Identity Theory (1979) and Japanese cross-cultural researcher who tested generalized reciprocity expectations inside minimal groups (SRC1).

Henri Tajfel (1919-1982) led the Bristol school alongside Michael Billig, R. P. Bundy, Claude Flament, and brilliant student John C. Turner (1947-2011), co-authoring **Social Identity Theory (1979)** — one of the most influential theories in all of social science.

## 4. The event / experiment, step by step

Tajfel et al. (1971, SRC1) categorized 64 Bristol boys (ages 14–15) ostensibly by dot-estimation style or Klee-vs-Kandinsky painting preference (actually random!), seating them alone to allocate real-money points between anonymous in-group and out-group code numbers via specially engineered **Payoff Matrices** that pitted **Maximum Joint Profit (19/25)** against **Maximum Difference (7/1)**.

1. **1971 Bristol School Cubicles: Klee vs Kandinsky Abstract Prints & Choosing '7-for-Us / 1-for-Them' Over '19-for-Us / 25-for-Them'.** (*1971, Department of Psychology, University of Bristol, UK*). The scene unfolds inside a sunlit 1971 British university room featuring two framed modernist abstract prints on an easel (**'Klee Group'** and **'Kandinsky Group'**), private wooden student desks, and a bold oversized **Tajfel Allocation Matrix Booklet** contrasting **Choice A (Max Joint Profit: 19 for Klee #47 / 25 for Kandinsky #23)** against **Choice B (Max Difference: 7 for Klee #47 / 1 for Kandinsky #23 — CIRCLED IN RED!)**. Henri Tajfel (wearing 1971 British tweed blazer, dark turtleneck/shirt, and spectacles) is standing beside the Klee & Kandinsky easel holding the Matrix tally sheet in stunned fascination as boys sacrifice 12 points just to outscore the other painter label; two Bristol Schoolboy (wearing neat 1971 British school blazers and striped ties) are seated in separate desks holding 'Group Klee' cards — firmly circling the **7-vs-1 Maximum Difference** box over the much richer **19-vs-25** box. The experimental setup features **paired abstract modernist art prints ('Klee' & 'Kandinsky') + random group ID badges** (meaningless aesthetic labels assigned 100% at random; Tajfel's iconic 'minimal' trigger), and **Tajfel Payoff Matrix Board ('19 / 25 Richer For Everyone [Skipped] vs 7 / 1 We Beat Them! [Chosen]')** (exposes relative-status tribalism with zero personal greed; the smoking-gun number table of Social Identity Theory). Henri Tajfel hands each boy a private 'Member of Klee Group' slip after viewing the two abstract paintings, then Boys inspect the Payoff Matrix, and finally bypass the rich 19/25 column and decisively circle 7/1 so Klee finishes +6 ahead of Kandinsky. In response, Henri Tajfel shakes his head realizing there IS no 'zero baseline' once humans hear 'Us' and 'Them'; the schoolboy sit proudly having 'won' for Klee despite never meeting a single fellow Klee member. Split schoolboys randomly by two abstract painters they've never heard of, and within five minutes they throw away 63% of their own team's money just so the other painter's group gets less (SRC1, SRC2, SRC_M1, SRC_M2, SRC_STILL).

2. **1973 Explicit Random Lottery: Even Pure Chance Creates a Tribe.** (*1973, Bristol laboratory (Billig & Tajfel)*). The setup centers on a bright classroom table where an experimenter draws slips openly out of a clear glass bowl labeled 'Random Group X / Random Group W'. Billig & Students are watching openly random Group X vs Group W assignments STILL produce clear in-group point favoritism. Central to the procedure is **clear glass random-draw bowl ('Group X / Group W')** (removes even the illusion of shared artistic taste; Billig & Tajfel 1973 escalation). The experimenter draws group X openly by chance, and then group X still awards more points to X than W. Immediately afterward, the researchers confirm bare linguistic categorization alone trips the switch. Tell people straight to their faces their group label was picked out of a hat at random — and five minutes later they STILL favor their hat letter (SRC2, SRC_M1, SRC_M2).

3. **Corporate '7-vs-1' Silos: Sales vs Engineering Turf Wars** (*Modern glass-walled office*). This phase takes place in a sunlit office split by two department banners ('Team Blue' vs 'Team Amber') hoarding information. Two managers are holding back helpful data so their own department ranks #1 on quarterly slides while overall project launch lags. Central to the procedure is **19/25 Company Win vs 7/1 Department Ranking slide** (shows Tajfel's exact 1971 Bristol matrix happening inside Fortune 500 offices; everyday workplace mirror). The managers spot the 7-vs-1 trap costing both teams. Across the room, the managers takes down the rival banners. Every time two company departments hoard credit so 'our silo wins' while the whole company loses, Bristol's 1971 schoolboys are running the meeting (SRC1, SRC2).

4. **The Antidote: Cross-Cutting Shared Mission Teams** (*Modern collaborative studio table*). The setup centers on a warm sunlit round oak table where engineers, designers, and marketers share one unified 'Launch Crew' badge and joint 19+25 scoreboard. The cross-functional teams are collaborating openly around a round table selecting the 44-point Maximum Joint Profit outcome together. Central to the procedure is **unified Common Ingroup badge & Joint Scoreboard** (harnesses Social Identity Theory FOR cooperation rather than zero-sum rivalry; gaertner & Dovidio solution). The team combines pieces into one shared launch board, and then everyone wins higher payout. At that moment, the team shared high-morale win. You can't delete the human tribal instinct — redraw the circle around a shared mission so 'Us' includes the whole table (SRC1, SRC2).

## 5. Results (numbers as reported)

Even with zero self-interest and zero interaction, boys consistently favored anonymous in-group members — and crucially **sacrificed absolute in-group profit (19 to 7) just to maximize relative victory over the out-group (7 vs 1)**. In 1973, Billig & Tajfel (SRC2) proved even *explicitly random* Group X / W draws sparked favoritism.

- Having survived Nazi POW camps while losing nearly his entire family in Poland, **Henri Tajfel** wanted to find the **true zero-point of prejudice**: strip away history, religion, competition, insults, and even seeing faces — then slowly ADD ingredients one by one until discrimination appeared. (SRC1)
- Except Tajfel **NEVER GOT TO STEP TWO** — because the 'empty baseline' itself exploded with instant tribalism (**Tajfel, Billig, Bundy & Flament, 1971, *European Journal of Social Psychology***). (SRC1)
- In Bristol, **64 schoolboys (ages 14–15)** were shown rapid clusters of dots to estimate ('Overestimators vs Underestimators' in Exp. 1) OR shown 12 abstract paintings by **Paul Klee and Wassily Kandinsky** (neither artist known to the boys, and secretly assigned **100% AT RANDOM** into **'Klee Group'** vs **'Kandinsky Group'** in Exp. 2!). (SRC1)
- Sitting alone in private cubicles with **zero personal self-interest** (a boy could NEVER award a single penny to himself — only to anonymous 'Member #47 of Klee' vs 'Member #23 of Kandinsky'!), the boys chose columns on clever **Payoff Matrices**. (SRC1)
- Look at the **stunner inside Matrix B**: Column 1 gave **19 points to your fellow Klee member and 25 points to the Kandinsky kid** (BIGGEST payout for your own teammate + biggest total wealth = 44!); Column 13 gave **only 7 points to your Klee teammate and 1 point to the Kandinsky kid**. (SRC1)
- Time after time, Bristol boys **sacrificed 12 points of their OWN teammate's real money** just to select **Maximum Difference (MD)** — choosing **7-vs-1 ('We beat Them by +6!')** over **19-vs-25 ('Everyone gets rich, yet They get more than Us!')**. (SRC1)
- Two years later (**Billig & Tajfel, 1973, SRC2**), they stripped away even the fake Klee/Kandinsky art excuse — telling participants straight to their faces they were split into **Group X and Group W purely by random chance** — and **in-group favoritism STILL emerged significantly**. (SRC2)
- Crucial modern nuance (Mummendey's **Positive-Negative Asymmetry** & Brewer 1999; Yamagishi, SRC3): in minimal groups, ordinary people eagerly hog *rewards and points* for 'Us' ('in-group love'), yet **hesitate to inflict direct physical pain or penalties** on 'Them' ('out-group hate') UNTIL scarce resources, threat, or dehumanizing leaders turn 'Us > Them' into hostility. (SRC3)

Several quantitative contrasts stood out across the published data. Henri Tajfel intended the Klee-vs-Kandinsky label to be the **boring '0% control baseline'** before adding real conflict — and watched 14-year-old boys turn two modernist painters they'd never heard of into rival tribes in **five minutes** — a striking result given that you don't need ancient hatred to start a tribe — two meaningless nouns do it in 300 seconds (SRC1). Give a 'Klee' boy a choice: **19 real-money points for your own Klee teammate + 25 for a Kandinsky kid**, OR **just 7 points for your Klee teammate + 1 for Kandinsky** — and majorities cheerfully **slash their own teammate's payout by 63% just so 'Klee' finishes ahead on the scoreboard** — notable because beating 'Them' mattered more than enriching 'Us' (SRC1). In 1973 (Billig & Tajfel, SRC2), experimenters literally told kids *'You were put in Group X purely by random draw — you have zero in common'* — and Group X STILL favored Group X — revealing how bare social categorization alone flips on 'Us vs Them' (SRC2). Remember the **Zero Self-Interest** rule: no boy could give a single point to HIMSELF — meaning pure greed couldn't explain a single penny of the bias — highlighting that Identity pride operating completely without personal pocketbook gain (SRC1).

## 6. Twist, controversy, misconceptions

Why does a random hat letter hijack rational self-interest? **Social Identity Theory** (SRC3): our brains tie personal self-worth partly to *how our group ranks*, turning arbitrary boundaries into instant status scoreboards. Yet modern researchers (Marilynn Brewer; Amélie Mummendey) uncovered a hopeful boundary: minimal labels spark *in-group reward favoritism* ('helping Klee first'), **NOT automatic cruelty** — ordinary people actually refuse to deal out painful penalties in minimal matrices until fear-mongering leaders add fake threat.

- **"Prejudice and 'us-vs-them' favoritism require decades of real historical grievances or scarce food."** — Tajfel's Minimal Group Paradigm proved mere cognitive categorization ('You are Group Klee') triggers instant favoritism and relative-advantage seeking within minutes — even when total pie shrinks. (SRC1)
- **"In-group love automatically equals violent out-group hatred."** — Mummendey and Marilynn Brewer proved a vital distinction: minimal labels spark *in-group reward favoritism*, whereas active *harming* of out-groups requires added threat, moral exclusion, or zero-sum propaganda. (SRC3)
- **"Smart adults never fall for meaningless 'Klee vs Kandinsky' tribes."** — Check Mac vs PC, iOS green-bubble vs blue-bubble texts, cross-department 'Sales vs Engineering' turf wars, or sports jerseys — educated adults play Matrix 7-vs-1 every Tuesday. (SRC3)

## 7. What happened after (follow-ups, modern science)

Tajfel's Minimal Group Paradigm explained everything from sports fandom and corporate department turf wars to green-bubble/blue-bubble phone tribalism — and birthed the **Common Ingroup Identity Model** for uniting divided teams.

Subsequent peer-reviewed studies tested, refined, and bounded these findings. **SRC2** (Michael Billig, Henri Tajfel (1973). Social categorization and similarity in intergroup behaviour. European Journal of Social Psychology) demonstrates significant ingroup favoritism even when group categorization is explicitly made by visible random coin toss. **SRC3** (Marilynn B. Brewer (1999). The Psychology of Prejudice: Ingroup Love and Outgroup Hate? Journal of Social Issues) clarifies that minimal-group discrimination primarily reflects preferential ingroup reward allocation ('ingroup love') rather than active outgroup hostility. **SRC5** (Michael Diehl (1990). The Minimal Group Paradigm: Theoretical Explanations and Empirical Findings. European Review of Social Psychology) offered a comprehensive review of minimal-group ingroup favoritism experiments and social-identity vs interdependence mechanisms. Further peer-reviewed analysis appeared in Sabine Otten (2011). Minimal Group Paradigm. The Encyclopedia of Peace Psychology (**SRC4**).

## 8. What it means for you

Searching for a zero-bias baseline at Bristol in 1971, Holocaust survivor Henri Tajfel discovered the Minimal Group Effect — assigning boys randomly to 'Klee' or 'Kandinsky' painting groups with zero contact or self-benefit caused them to sacrifice total group wealth (picking 7-vs-1 over 19-vs-25) simply so 'Us' came out ahead of 'Them'. Audit every label you hand out at work or school: arbitrary silos ('Day Shift vs Night Shift', 'HQ vs Field') automatically breed zero-sum 7-vs-1 turf wars — unite people in **cross-cutting, shared-goal teams** so 'Us' expands.

Watch how fast arbitrary labels ('Engineering vs Sales', 'Us vs Them') trick smart adults into playing **7-vs-1** instead of **19-vs-25** — mix cross-functional teams around one shared goal.

Core scientific concepts from this research translate into everyday terms as follows:

- **Minimal Group Paradigm (1971)**: stripping groups down to nothing but an arbitrary label (Klee vs Kandinsky), anonymity, and zero self-profit — yet still observing robust in-group favoritism — comparable to finding sparks inside an empty test tube.
- **Maximum Difference (7/1) vs Maximum Joint Profit (19/25)**: Tajfel's matrix trap proving people sacrifice total wealth and even their own group's absolute payout just to rank higher than the out-group — comparable to preferring $7 when rivals get $1 over $19 when rivals get $25.
- **Social Identity Theory (Tajfel & Turner 1979)**: principle that part of our self-esteem comes from the groups we belong to — pushing us to make 'our group' positively distinct — comparable to polishing our team trophy to feel taller ourselves.
- **in-group love vs out-group hate asymmetry**: finding that minimal labels readily trigger unequal *helping/rewards* for 'Us' while resisting direct *cruelty* unless threat is added — comparable to cheering louder for home jerseys without wanting rivals hurt.
- **superordinate recategorization**: merging rival 'Klee' and 'Kandinsky' sub-tribes under one shared mission identity ('Common Ingroup Identity', Gaertner & Dovidio) — comparable to rival club players putting on the same national team jersey.

In daily life, this mechanism surfaces in familiar situations:

- Laughing at how fast people judge a stranger's text bubble color, phone brand, or airline boarding group.
- Watching two corporate departments ('Product' vs 'Marketing') quietly withhold credit so 'our team looks better' even when the whole company loses revenue (19/25 vs 7/1 in a suit!).
- Dissolving turf wars instantly by **re-drawing one shared superordinate circle** ('Cross-Functional Launch Team Alpha') instead of two departmental silos.
