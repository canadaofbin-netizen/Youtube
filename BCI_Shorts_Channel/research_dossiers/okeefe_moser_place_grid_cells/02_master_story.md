# O'Keefe (1971) & May-Britt and Edvard Moser (2005): Place Cells, Hexagonal Grid Cells & Your Brain's Inner GPS — Master Story (longform depth)

## 1. Cold open (the iconic moment)

In 1971 at University College London, **John O'Keefe** did something radical: instead of bolting an animal still in front of flashing lights, he unplugged the restraints, let it wander freely around an open table, and listened to single cells in its **hippocampus** (SRC1). Cell #1 stayed completely silent until the animal stepped into the northwest corner — *pop-pop-pop!* — acting like a literal **'You Are Here' pin**. Thirty-four years later in Trondheim, Norway, **May-Britt and Edvard Moser** widened the arena walls to two meters, peered one synapse upstream into **entorhinal cortex**, and gasped as single brain cells drew **mathematically crisp 60-degree hexagonal honeycomb graph paper** across the entire floor — winning all three the **2014 Nobel Prize** (SRC2).

Recorded in 1971 London & 2005 Trondheim Nobel Spatial Neuroscience Suite, the defining visual record centers on a sunlit neurobiology lab with a 2-meter white open-field floor holding a rat beside dual trajectory screens comparing a single-spot Place Cell and a 60-degree hexagonal Grid Cell honeycomb (SRC1, SRC_STILL). John O'Keefe's 1971 discovery of hippocampal 'Place Cells' ('you are here') and May-Britt and Edvard Moser's 2005 discovery of entorhinal 'Grid Cells' (a 60-degree hexagonal coordinate ruler) revealed the brain's internal GPS — winning the 2014 Nobel Prize and showing how spatial maps also organize human memories and ideas (SRC1).

## 2. World before (era, place, why it mattered)

Strict behaviorists insisted navigation was just a blind chain of 'turn left, then turn right' muscle habits, mocking Edward Tolman's 1948 proposal that brains carry an internal 'Cognitive Map'.

At University College London (1971) & Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU), Trondheim (2005) in 1971, **John O'Keefe & Dostrovsky's 1971 Hippocampal Place Cells + May-Britt & Edvard Moser's 2005 Entorhinal Hexagonal Grid Cells (2014 Nobel Prize)** turned an unresolved question about human mind and brain function into a controlled, reproducible empirical test (SRC1: J. O'Keefe, J. Dostrovsky (1971). The hippocampus as a spatial map. Preliminary evidence from unit activity in the freely-moving rat. Brain Research).

## 3. People

- **John O'Keefe**: UCL neuroscientist (mentored under Ronald Melzack at McGill) who let freely moving animals explore open arenas in 1971 and heard single hippocampal cells fire strictly at ONE spot in the room — vindicating Tolman's cognitive map (SRC1).
- **May-Britt Moser & Edvard I. Moser**: Trondheim neuroscientist duo (former trainees with O'Keefe) who expanded the arena floor in 2005 and discovered entorhinal 'Grid Cells' tiling all of space in breathtaking 60-degree equilateral triangles / hexagons (SRC1).
- **Jonathan Dostrovsky & Eleanor Maguire / Joshua Jacobs**: 1971 co-discoverer at UCL and human neuroimaging/intracranial researchers who confirmed place and 6-fold grid codes inside the human brain — even for abstract ideas (SRC1).

New York–born John O'Keefe (b. 1939, UCL) discovered hippocampal Place Cells with Jonathan Dostrovsky in 1971; Norwegian psychologists-turned-neurobiologists May-Britt Moser (b. 1963) and Edvard I. Moser (b. 1962) trained briefly in O'Keefe's London lab before building their powerhouse Trondheim institute and discovering Grid Cells in 2005.

## 4. The event / experiment, step by step

Recording single-unit spikes during free open-field foraging while tracking overhead x-y coordinates — and testing landmark rotations, darkness, and enlarged 2-meter enclosures (O'Keefe & Dostrovsky 1971, SRC1; Moser et al. 2005/2015, SRC2) — researchers mapped how hippocampus and medial entorhinal cortex encode space.

## 5. Results (numbers as reported)

Hippocampal **Place Cells** fired in unique single locations; rotating visual landmarks rotated the map; and medial entorhinal **Grid Cells** tiled all of space in periodic **60° hexagonal lattices** alongside Head-Direction compass cells and Border wall cells (SRC1, SRC2).

- In 1948 Edward Tolman claimed brains build internal **'Cognitive Maps'**, while behaviorists scoffed that 'maps in the head' were unscientific fairy tales. In **1971 (*Brain Research*)**, **John O'Keefe** and student **Jonathan Dostrovsky** at University College London listened to single CA1 **hippocampal neurons** in freely exploring animals — instead of strapping heads still. (SRC1)
- O'Keefe discovered **8 neurons** unlike anything ever recorded: Cell 1 ignored food, water, grooming, and sound anywhere else, firing **exclusively whenever the animal walked through the NORTH-WEST corner**; Cell 2 fired strictly by the **SOUTH edge**; Cell 3 strictly at **CENTER** — **'Place Cells'** whose ensemble literally spelled out *'Where am I right now?'*. (SRC1)
- Even in total darkness after landmarks are switched off, Place Cells keep firing in the right spot using self-motion step integration — and if you rotate the room's visual cue card by 90 degrees, the entire hippocampal map rotates 90 degrees with it. (SRC1)
- For **34 years** a huge question remained: WHERE does the hippocampus get its metric ruler for distance and direction? In **2005** in Trondheim, Norway, **May-Britt and Edvard Moser** recorded one synapse upstream in **dorsomedial entorhinal cortex** — and made a crucial experimental tweak: **they built a MUCH LARGER 2-meter circular/square arena** so patterns wouldn't get cropped. (SRC2)
- As spikes plotted across the larger floor, the Trondheim lab gasped: a single entorhinal **Grid Cell** didn't fire at just one spot — it fired across **dozens of regularly spaced dots arranged in a mathematically crisp 60-degree hexagonal / equilateral-triangle honeycomb lattice** tiling the entire room like invisible graph paper. (SRC2)
- Alongside **Head-Direction Cells** (internal compass, Ranck/Taube) and **Border/Boundary Cells** (wall rulers), Grid Cells and Place Cells form a complete **Internal GPS** — winning O'Keefe, May-Britt Moser, and Edvard Moser the **2014 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine**. (SRC2)
- Does the **human brain** have Place and Grid cells? **YES!** Intracranial recordings in awake neurosurgical patients driving virtual taxis (Ekstrom 2003; Jacobs 2013) and 6-fold fMRI symmetry signals (Doeller, Barry & Burgess 2010) confirmed both Place Cells and 60° hexagonal Grid coding in humans. (SRC3)
- Modern mind-expanding twist (Constantinescu, Behrens 2016, SRC3): human entorhinal Grid Cells don't just map physical streets — they use the **exact same 60-degree hexagonal coordinate grid to navigate abstract 'concept spaces'** (social hierarchies, bird shapes, and ideas), explaining why **entorhinal cortex** is the #1 earliest site where Alzheimer's disease causes both getting lost AND losing episodic orientation. (SRC3)
- Following O'Keefe and Dostrovsky's (1971) hippocampal place cells and Moser et al.'s (2005, reviewed 2015) medial entorhinal grid cells, cognitive neuroscience linked the 2,500-year-old (2500-year) classical Method of Loci to medial temporal spatial scaffolding. (SRC1)

Several quantitative contrasts stood out across the published data. At first the Mosers saw just three or four dots in a small box — only when they **built a giant 2-meter arena floor** did a stunning **60-degree crystal honeycomb hexagon** leap onto the computer monitor, generated spontaneously by single brain cells — a striking result given that sometimes a Nobel Prize is hiding six inches outside a too-small test box (SRC2). Immanuel Kant argued in 1781 that the human mind comes pre-equipped with an innate framework of 'Space' before experience — O'Keefe and the Mosers found the literal **hexagonal graph paper and 'You-Are-Here' pins** inside entorhinal-hippocampal circuits — notable because 200 years of philosophy answered by single-neuron recordings (SRC1). Watch a sleeping brain right after learning a maze: hippocampal Place Cells **replay the exact daytime path at 10x fast-forward speed** during slow-wave sleep — and even simulate **unchosen future paths** before turning a corner — revealing how your GPS replays yesterday and previews tomorrow (SRC2). Why is getting turned around in a familiar neighborhood often the **very first subtle sign of Alzheimer's disease** years before severe memory loss? Because tau tangles strike **entorhinal Grid-Cell cortex** first — highlighting that grid-cell neuroanatomy explains early clinical diagnosis (SRC2).

## 6. Twist, controversy, misconceptions

Why did Grid Cells hide until 2005? Because inside standard small 60-cm lab boxes you only see two or three vertices of the hexagon — widening the box to **2 meters** let the breathtaking honeycomb repeat across the screen. Even cooler, human intracranial recordings and fMRI (Constantinescu et al. 2016, SRC3) revealed our human entorhinal cortex reuses that **exact 60-degree hexagonal GPS to map abstract ideas and social relationships** — AND explained why early Alzheimer's pathology in entorhinal cortex causes subtle spatial disorientation years before memory clinics usually test for it.

- **"Your hippocampus only cares about memorizing vocabulary lists and birthdays."** — Spatial cognitive mapping ('Where am I?') was the ancient evolutionary job of the mammalian hippocampus — humans co-opted that exact spatial Place-and-Grid machinery to index *episodic memories* and *abstract concepts*. (SRC1)
- **"Relying 100% on turn-by-turn smartphone GPS arrows exercises your hippocampal map."** — Turn-by-turn 'turn left in 50 feet' engages striatal habit following while leaving hippocampal spatial mapping idle; glancing at an overview map and orienting landmarks wakes your Place & Grid network up. (SRC2)
- **"Grid cells depend on painted lines on the floor."** — The hexagonal lattice is generated *internally* by attractor dynamics inside medial entorhinal cortex — persisting even in pitch darkness. (SRC2)

## 7. What happened after (follow-ups, modern science)

O'Keefe and the Mosers shared the 2014 Nobel Prize, transforming spatial navigation, early Alzheimer's diagnostics (VR grid-cell navigation tests!), robotics SLAM algorithms, and episodic memory theory.

Subsequent peer-reviewed studies tested, refined, and bounded these findings. **SRC3** (Alexandra O. Constantinescu, Jill X. O'Reilly, Timothy E. J. Behrens (2016). At the neural-circuit level, the **temporal lobe** (medial entorhinal 60° hexagonal Grid ruler) contributes by tiling every step across the open 2-meter floor in periodic equilateral-triangle / honeycomb coordinates, which captures May-Britt & Edvard Moser's 2005 Trondheim Grid-Cell discovery; meanwhile, the **hippocampus** (single-spot CA1 Place Cell pin ('You Are Here!')) contributes by combining entorhinal grid metrics with room landmarks to fire sharply ONLY inside one specific corner of the arena, which captures John O'Keefe's 1971 UCL Place-Cell discovery; meanwhile, the **prefrontal cortex** (goal route planning & Memory Palace indexing) contributes by reading hippocampal place-and-grid trajectories to plan future detours and organize abstract human concepts, which links spatial GPS to human planning and episodic memory.

## 8. What it means for you

John O'Keefe's 1971 discovery of hippocampal 'Place Cells' ('you are here') and May-Britt and Edvard Moser's 2005 discovery of entorhinal 'Grid Cells' (a 60-degree hexagonal coordinate ruler) revealed the brain's internal GPS — winning the 2014 Nobel Prize and showing how spatial maps also organize human memories and ideas. Wake up your hippocampal-entorhinal GPS in daily life: orient yourself to real landmarks before turning on turn-by-turn navigation, and place tough facts inside vivid 'Memory Palace' rooms so Place Cells lock them in.

Don't let turn-by-turn GPS arrows put your hippocampus to sleep: orient yourself to North/South landmarks when walking new streets, and store important speeches inside a spatial 'Memory Palace'.

Core scientific concepts from this research translate into everyday terms as follows:

- **Place Cells (O'Keefe 1971)**: hippocampal neurons that fire briskly whenever you occupy one specific location ('by the window') in a familiar environment — comparable to 'you Are Here' pushpins on your brain's map.
- **Grid Cells (Mosers 2005)**: medial entorhinal cortex neurons firing across a periodic 60-degree hexagonal honeycomb lattice tiling all of navigable space — comparable to invisible latitude-and-longitude graph paper measuring distance walked.
- **Head-Direction & Border Cells**: companion neurons tracking which compass way your head faces and how close walls sit — comparable to the compass needle and ruler completing the brain's GPS.
- **hippocampal replay**: 10x fast-forward firing sequences of Place Cells during rest and sleep consolidating newly walked routes and episodes — comparable to nighttime dashcam fast-forward saving today's journey.
- **conceptual grid coding**: human entorhinal cortex reusing 60-degree hexagonal grids to map non-physical relationships and ideas — comparable to navigating thoughts on the same graph paper used for streets.

In daily life, this mechanism surfaces in familiar situations:

- Using the ancient 'Memory Palace' (*Method of Loci*) — placing grocery items or speech points along rooms of your childhood house — and realizing why it works like magic: you are stapling facts directly onto hippocampal **Place Cells**.
- Glancing at North/South landmarks before walking through a new city instead of staring only at a blue turn-by-turn arrow.
- Understanding why we use spatial metaphors for *everything* ('a close friend', 'a high position', 'distant cousins') — our entorhinal Grid Cells literally map social ideas on 60° coordinates.
