# Cosmides & Tooby (1989): Why Humans Flunk Abstract Logic (20%) Yet Catch Free-Riders Instantly (75%)! — Master Story (longform depth)

## 1. Cold open (the iconic moment)

Why can highly educated adults stare at a simple four-card logic puzzle (*'If a card has a vowel on one side, it has an even number on the other'*) and **fail 80% of the time** — yet spot within **half a second** at a restaurant table when one friend orders lobster and tries to split the bill evenly? In **1989 (*Cognition*)**, **Leda Cosmides** (working with **John Tooby**) tested an unfamiliar tribal rule — ***'If a man eats cassava root, then he must have a tattoo on his face'*** — and watched accuracy **skyrocket from ~23% to 75%** the instant Cassava Root became a **rationed perk** (SRC1).

Recorded in 1989, Department of Psychology, Stanford University, California, the defining visual record centers on a sunlit 1989 Stanford lab table displaying four oversized 'Eats Cassava Root / Eats Molo Nuts / Has Tattoo / No Tattoo' cards beside a 23%-to-75% Cheater-Detection scoreboard as students flip the two cheater cards (SRC1, SRC_STILL). In 1989, Leda Cosmides (with John Tooby) proved that humans fail abstract 'If P then Q' logic ~80% of the time yet leap to ~75% accuracy on unfamiliar rules ('cassava root to facial tattoo') the instant they involve catching a Social-Contract Cheater who took a rationed benefit without paying the cost (SRC1).

## 2. World before (era, place, why it mattered)

Cognitive science treated the human mind like a general-purpose desktop computer running content-blind formal logic (*If P then Q*) — blaming errors on unfamiliarity when people solved 'Drinking Beer / Over 21' easily.

At Stanford University & Harvard University (published in Cognition, 1989) in 1989, **Leda Cosmides's 1989 Stanford / Harvard 'Logic of Social Exchange' Cassava-Root Cheater-Detection Experiments** turned an unresolved question about human mind and brain function into a controlled, reproducible empirical test (SRC1: Leda Cosmides (1989). The logic of social exchange: Has natural selection shaped how humans reason? Studies with the Wason selection task. Cognition).

## 3. People

- **Leda Cosmides & John Tooby**: Cognitive psychologist and evolutionary anthropologist who co-founded Evolutionary Psychology and tested whether human reasoning shifts completely when catching social-contract free-riders (SRC1).
- **Stanford & Harvard Student Problem-Solvers**: Undergraduates who flunked unfamiliar descriptive 4-card rules (<25%) yet leaped to **~75% accuracy** on the EXACT SAME unfamiliar words (*Cassava Root / Molo Nuts / Facial Tattoo*) the moment the story framed Cassava Root as a **rationed Benefit** (SRC1).
- **Valerie Stone, Patient R.M., Patricia Cheng & Keith Holyoak**: Neuropsychologists who tested bilateral orbitofrontal/amygdala lesion **Patient R.M.** (intact hazard-safety cards, selectively impaired social-cheater cards, *PNAS* 2002!) and cognitive critics who proposed 'Pragmatic Permission Schemas' (SRC5).

Leda Cosmides and John Tooby united Robert Trivers's **Reciprocal Altruism** (cooperation collapses unless you detect free-riders!) with experimental cognitive psychology and, later with Valerie Stone and Robert Knight, lesion neuropsychology.

## 4. The event / experiment, step by step

In *Cognition* (1989, SRC1), Cosmides held the unfamiliar 4-card rule (*Eats Cassava Root | Eats Molo Nuts | Tattoo | No Tattoo*) **100% word-for-word identical** across two background stories: a **Descriptive Story** (neutral geographic correlation) vs a **Social Contract Story** (cassava is a prized ration earned only by enduring initiation tattooing). In **Experiments 3–4**, she tested **Switched Social Contracts** (*'If a man pays the cost, he gets the benefit'*), where formal logic and cheater-detection predict **opposite card pairs**.

1. **1989 Stanford 4-Card Table: Same 'Cassava Root & Tattoo' Cards Jump From 23% (Neutral Fact) to 75% (Catch the Free-Rider!)** (*1989, Department of Psychology, Stanford University, California*). The scene unfolds inside a sunlit 1989 Stanford cognitive laboratory featuring a clean oak table holding **4 oversized two-sided white test cards** (**'Eats Cassava Root' | 'Eats Molo Nuts' | 'Has Tattoo' | 'No Tattoo'**) paired with a bold split **Context Comparison Board**: **Left = Descriptive Geography Story (23% Correct)** vs **Right = Rationed Benefit Social Contract Story (75% Correct — 'Eats Cassava' + 'No Tattoo' Flipped!)**. Leda Cosmides (wearing 1989 tailored blazer and crisp collared blouse) is standing beside the 4-card oak table swapping the background story placard from 'Descriptive Geography' to 'Rationed Benefit Social Contract' without touching a single word on the 4 cards; two Stanford Student (wearing late-1980s collegiate sweaters) are staring baffled at the Descriptive story (23%), then instantly reaching out and flipping **'Eats Cassava Root'** + **'No Tattoo'** at **75% accuracy** the moment Cassava becomes an earned Benefit. The experimental setup features **4 oversized Wason test cards ('Eats Cassava Root' | 'Eats Molo Nuts' | 'Has Tattoo' | 'No Tattoo')** (keeps every word of the unfamiliar rule 100% constant across conditions; Cosmides 1989's brilliant experimental control), and **Story-Frame Flip Board ('Neutral Fact: 23% | Catch the Benefit-Without-Cost Cheater: 75%!')** (makes the 3x reasoning leap unmistakable at a glance; anchors Cognition 1989 data). Leda Cosmides lays down the 4 unfamiliar Cassava/Tattoo cards under a neutral geography story, then students hesitate and miss 'No Tattoo' (77% fail), then Cosmides swaps ONLY the story frame to 'Rationed Benefit for Initiates', and finally students snap straight to 'Eats Cassava' + 'No Tattoo' (75% pass). In response, the students grin amazed at how effortless the exact same 4 cards became once framed as 'Who skipped paying?'; Leda Cosmides logs the Switched-Contract not-P & Q proof right beside it. Keep the four unfamiliar 'Cassava Root and Tattoo' cards 100% identical: tell students it's a geography fact and 77% fail — tell them Cassava is a rationed perk and 75% catch the freeloader in seconds (SRC1, SRC2, SRC_M1, SRC_M2, SRC_STILL).

2. **The Switched-Contract Stunner: Breaking Aristotle's Logic to Catch Cheaters.** (*1989, Stanford seminar table (Cosmides Exp. 3 & 4)*). The setup centers on a bright seminar table showing 'If Paid Cost to Gets Benefit' where 71% flip 'Did NOT Pay Cost (not-P)' + 'Took Benefit (Q)' instead of formal textbook P & not-Q. Cosmides & Students are watching smart humans violate textbook propositional logic to track Benefit-Without-Cost. Central to the procedure is **Formal Logic (P & not-Q) vs Social Exchange (not-P & Q: 71%!) card** (decisive disproof of pure formal-logic models; Cosmides 1989 Exp. 3–4). The students bypass P & not-Q and flip not-P & Q to catch the unpaid benefit-taker. Immediately afterward, Cosmides confirms content-specialized social reasoning. Reverse the wording of a social contract and 71% of humans cheerfully break formal textbook logic just to keep catching the guy who took the perk without paying (SRC1, SRC_M1, SRC_M2).

3. **Patient R.M. (2002 PNAS): Separating Hazard Safety From Cheater Detection in the Brain** (*2002, Neuropsychology gallery (Stone, Cosmides & Tooby)*). This phase takes place in a well-lit studio gallery showing translucent 3D brain ventromedial prefrontal + amygdala pathways alongside Safety Precaution Cards (70% Intact) vs Social Cheater Cards (39% Impaired). The neuropsychologist & patient R.M. participants are solving 4-card TB-Mask Safety rules at 70% while dropping to 39% on 4-card Social Contract rules. Central to the procedure is **Safety Precaution (70%) vs Social Cheater (39%) Dissociation Board** (neurological double-check of specialized social-exchange circuitry; Stone et al. 2002 PNAS). Patient R.M. solves Safety Precaution 4-card rule cleanly, and then struggles selectively on identical Social Exchange 4-card rule. Across the room, the neuropsychologists map ventromedial prefrontal + amygdala role. After bilateral orbitofrontal and amygdala injury, Patient R.M. still aced 4-card Safety Hazard puzzles at 70% — while selectively losing 4-card Cheater puzzles (SRC5).

4. **Writing Clear Rules & Cooling False 'Freeloader' Alarms** (*Modern sunlit team office*). Positioned inside a bright collaborative whiteboard translating muddy 'If Tier A to Clause B' prose into crystal-clear 'Benefit Shared Contribution Expected' agreements. The team lead & Colleague are aligning clearly on shared contributions and checking facts before assuming malice. Central to the procedure is **Clear Benefit Contribution Charter** (turns 23% confusion into 75%+ instant clarity; actionable workplace tool). The team lead rewrites abstract rule into clear Benefit-for-Contribution terms, and then whole team understands on read #1. At that moment, the team shows clear, fair cooperation. Want people to understand complex rules on the first read? Stop writing like a logic textbook and frame clear Benefits alongside fair Contributions (SRC1, SRC2).

## 5. Results (numbers as reported)

Descriptive framing sank to **~21–25%** accuracy, while Social Contract framing jumped to **~75%**. Better still, on **Switched Social Contracts**, **~71% chose NOT-P and Q** — breaking formal textbook logic to catch the person taking the benefit without paying the cost (SRC1).

- Remember **Peter Wason's 4-Card Logic Trap** (*'If a card has a vowel on one side, it has an even number on the other'* — Cards **E, K, 4, 7**)? Fewer than **10–25% of university students** turn over **E and 7 (P and not-Q)** — yet when Griggs & Cox (1982) changed it to **'If a person is drinking beer, they must be over 21'**, **~75%** nailed **Beer + 16-Year-Old**. (SRC1)
- Skeptics said: *'Nothing special about cheating. People just know American drinking laws from real-world memory!'* So in **1989 (*Cognition*)**, **Leda Cosmides** designed a **brilliant 100%-unfamiliar test** nobody in America had ever experienced: **'If a man eats cassava root, then he must have a tattoo on his face'** — Cards: **Eats Cassava Root | Eats Molo Nuts | Tattoo | No Tattoo**. (SRC1)
- Look at how Cosmides held the **exact 14-word rule and 4 cards 100% IDENTICAL** while changing ONLY the background story. (SRC1)
- In the **Descriptive Folklore Story** (tattooed married men happen to live near cassava groves, so eating cassava correlates with having a tattoo), Stanford students scored down around **21–25%** — flunking just like abstract vowels and numbers. (SRC1)
- In the **Social Contract Story** (cassava root is a prized, scarce delicacy rationed ONLY to men who endured the painful initiation ritual of getting a facial tattoo!), **75% of Stanford students INSTANTLY flipped 'Eats Cassava Root' + 'No Tattoo'** — catching the guy eating the prized root without paying the price. (SRC1)
- Then Cosmides ran the **ULTIMATE killer experiment against formal logic (Switched Social Contract, Experiments 3 & 4, SRC1)**: *'If a man **pays the cost** (has a facial tattoo), then the tribe **must give him cassava root**'* — mathematically, formal logic *modus tollens* says flip **P & not-Q**. Instead, **~71% of humans flipped NOT-P ('No Tattoo') and Q ('Eats Cassava Root')** — VIOLATING formal propositional logic to catch the **Benefit-Without-Cost Cheater**. (SRC1)
- Thirteen years later (**Stone, Cosmides, Tooby, Kroll & Knight, 2002 *PNAS***), bilateral **ventromedial/orbitofrontal + amygdala** injury patient **R.M.** provided stunning neurological double-dissociation: R.M. scored **70% normal** on identically structured **Safety/Precaution 4-card rules** (*'If you work with toxic TB patients, you must wear a surgical mask'*), yet plunged to **39% impaired** specifically on **Social-Contract Cheater rules**. (SRC5)
- Balanced scientific debate (**Cheng & Holyoak 'Pragmatic Permission Schemas'; Sperber & Girotti relevance critique; Corning SRC3**): critics argue general **deontic permission/obligation rules** and conversational relevance contribute alongside evolutionary social-exchange pressures — yet everyone agrees **human reasoning rockets from ~20% to ~75% the moment an abstract rule becomes 'Who took the benefit without paying the cost?'**. (SRC1)
- In Cosmides's (1989) social-exchange experiments, roughly 75–80% of participants failed abstract descriptive Wason rules yet jumped to ~75% logical violation detection on unfamiliar 'cassava root and facial tattoo' social contracts. (SRC1)

Several quantitative contrasts stood out across the published data. Same 4 cards (**Eats Cassava | Eats Molo Nuts | Tattoo | No Tattoo**), same 0% prior American familiarity: frame it as a neutral geography fact and **75%+ fail**; frame Cassava as a **rationed Benefit earned by paying a Cost** and **75% solve it in seconds** — a striking result given that familiarity didn't solve the puzzle — flipping on the 'Free-Rider Radar' solved it (SRC1). In Cosmides's **Switched Social Contract**, formal textbook logic demands flipping **P and not-Q** — yet **71% of smart adults flip NOT-P and Q instead**, breaking textbook logic to catch **'Took Benefit + Paid Zero Cost'** — notable because proof positive that human minds run Social-Exchange logic, NOT Aristotle's truth tables (SRC1). Neurological **Patient R.M.** (limbic orbitofrontal + amygdala damage) aced 4-card **Safety Hazard** puzzles ('Exposed to Hazard without Protection' = 70%) while selectively failing 4-card **Social Cheater** puzzles (39%) — revealing how brain injury separated 'Watch out for danger!' from 'Watch out for freeloaders!' despite identical card logic (SRC1).

## 6. Twist, controversy, misconceptions

In **2002 (*PNAS*)**, bilateral **ventromedial prefrontal + amygdala** lesion patient **R.M.** scored **70% intact** on 4-card **Hazard/Safety** rules (*'If exposed to TB, wear a mask'*) while dropping to **39%** on **Social-Contract Cheater** rules. Even as cognitive researchers (**Cheng & Holyoak** permission schemas; **Corning, SRC3**) debate how specialized vs deontic the underlying mechanism is, the behavioral fact is rock-solid: **human brains wake up 3x sharper around fair social exchange**.

- **"People only solve the Drinking-Age 4-card puzzle because they've visited bars and seen ID checks in real life."** — Cosmides's unfamiliar *Cassava Root & Facial Tattoo* rule (SRC1) had zero prior real-world memory in California — yet jumped from ~23% to 75% purely by framing it as Benefit-for-Cost. (SRC1)
- **"Humans are obsessed with catching ALL rule-breakers equally — including generous altruists who pay the cost and skip the reward."** — Cosmides tested **'Altruist Detection'** (paid the cost, didn't take cassava!) — people rarely flip those cards. Our radar locks specifically onto **Took the Benefit + Dodged the Cost**. (SRC1)
- **"To teach complex policies or contracts, write them in neutral abstract 'If Condition A, then Requirement B' prose."** — Abstract prose drops human accuracy near 20% — rewriting policies clearly in **'To receive Benefit X, you must complete Requirement Y'** triples comprehension. (SRC1)

## 7. What happened after (follow-ups, modern science)

Cosmides (1989) became the flagship experimental paper of Evolutionary Psychology, reshaping contract law clarity, organizational fairness research, and social cognitive neuroscience.

Subsequent peer-reviewed studies tested, refined, and bounded these findings. **SRC5** (Valerie E. Stone, Leda Cosmides, John Tooby, Neal Kroll et al. (2002). Selective impairment of reasoning about social exchange in a patient with bilateral limbic system damage. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences) offered Stone, Cosmides, Tooby, Kroll & Knight (2002 PNAS) neuropsychological dissociation showing selective impairment of social-contract reasoning with intact precaution rules after bilateral limbic damage. **SRC6** (D Sperber (1995). Relevance theory explains the selection task. Cognition) provided Sperber, Cara & Girotto (1995 Cognition) Relevance Theory critique arguing pragmatic relevance cues explain thematic Wason selection facilitation without an innate cheater module. Further peer-reviewed replication, critique, and synthesis appeared across Dan Sperber, Vittorio Girotto (2002). Use or misuse of the selection task? Rejoinder to Fiddick, Cosmides, and Tooby. Cognition (**SRC2**); David Premack (1996). Correction to Premack Review of Barkow, Cosmides, and Tooby. Contemporary Psychology: A Journal of Reviews (**SRC3**); and Julio Muñoz Rubio (2018). John Tooby y Leda Cosmides: psicología evolutiva y falsa interdisciplina. Metatheoria – Revista de Filosofía e Historia de la Ciencia (**SRC4**).

At the neural-circuit level, **dorsolateral prefrontal cortex** (effortful formal 'If P then Q' abstract rule buffer) contributes by struggling at ~21–25% accuracy when juggling arbitrary descriptive rules ('vowel to even number' or folklore cassava geography) in working memory, which explains why standard abstract Wason rules stump 3 out of 4 adults; meanwhile, the **amygdala** (rapid salience tag for unfair 'Benefit Taken Without Cost') contributes by flagging the asymmetric free-rider threat essential to Trivers's reciprocal cooperation (injured bilaterally alongside orbitofrontal cortex in Patient R.M.!), which supplies affective social-exchange vigilance; meanwhile, **ventromedial prefrontal cortex** (social-contract Benefit-vs-Cost valuation (~75% accuracy!)) contributes by computing 'Who received the rationed Cassava Benefit without paying the Tattoo Cost?' effortlessly — even overriding formal textbook logic on switched rules, which matches Stone, Cosmides & Tooby (2002 PNAS) lesion dissociation.

## 8. What it means for you

In 1989, Leda Cosmides (with John Tooby) proved that humans fail abstract 'If P then Q' logic ~80% of the time yet leap to ~75% accuracy on unfamiliar rules ('cassava root to facial tattoo') the instant they involve catching a Social-Contract Cheater who took a rationed benefit without paying the cost. Translate dry abstract policies, study formulas, and team agreements into concrete **'Benefit Received Contribution Owed'** terms so everyone's 75% intuitive radar switches on — and remember our hairline free-rider alarm can over-fire on honest misunderstandings.

Rewrite confusing rules into explicit **Benefit Contribution** pairs so people stop making 80% abstract errors — and because your built-in free-rider radar is hair-trigger sensitive, always ask *'Did my teammate miss an email?'* before yelling *'Cheater!'*

Core scientific concepts from this research translate into everyday terms as follows:

- **Social Contract Algorithm (Cosmides 1989)**: specialized reasoning procedure ('If you take Benefit B, you must satisfy Requirement R') tuned to check Benefit-Taken ('Cassava') and Cost-Not-Paid ('No Tattoo') — comparable to a mental turnstile checking who hopped the gate without a ticket.
- **Cassava-Root unfamiliarity control**: proving 75% accuracy works on a rule no participant ever lived through in real life — ruling out simple rote memory — comparable to aceing a free-rider test on an imaginary island.
- **Switched Social Contract proof**: flipping not-P and Q (~71%) instead of formal logic's P and not-Q when the phrasing reverses ('If you pay Cost, you get Benefit') — comparable to following the free lunch instead of following the 'If-Then' grammar.
- **Patient R.M. dissociation (Stone et al. 2002)**: selective impairment on social-cheater cards (39%) despite preserved hazard-precaution cards (70%) after orbitofrontal + amygdala damage — comparable to two separate circuit breakers inside identical 4-card puzzles.

In daily life, this mechanism surfaces in familiar situations:

- Glazing over completely while reading an abstract software license or tax form — yet spotting within **0.4 seconds** when one coworker takes shared project credit after skipping every weekend shift.
- Rewriting confusing team SOPs or customer policies into crisp **Benefit to Required Step** format so people stop making 80% errors.
- Checking your own blind spot: because your brain hunts *other* people's unpaid benefits at 75% accuracy, pause to audit whether YOU quietly undercounted a teammate's contribution.
