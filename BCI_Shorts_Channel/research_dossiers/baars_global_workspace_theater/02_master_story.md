# Bernard Baars (1988-2005): Global Workspace Theory — How a Single Conscious 'Stage' Broadcasts to Billions of Unconscious Specialists — Master Story (longform depth)

## 1. Cold open (the iconic moment)

Your brain packs 86 billion neurons firing in massively parallel harmony — balancing your posture, parsing English grammar, and regulating blood pressure all at once without breaking a sweat (SRC1). So why on Earth do you go completely deaf to a podcast the instant you read a two-line text message? In 1988, neurobiologist Bernard Baars solved that riddle with one brilliant architectural insight: consciousness isn't the factory floor — it's the factory's single PA microphone.

Recorded in 1988-2005, San Diego cognitive neuroscience seminar laboratory, the defining visual record centers on a cognitive neuroscientist beside a 'Global Workspace' broadcast diagram while a participant looks into a two-card 'House vs Face' tabletop stereoscope (SRC1, SRC_STILL). Bernard Baars explained why you can only think one conscious thought at a time inside an 86-billion-neuron brain: consciousness acts like a central PA microphone that broadcasts ONE winning problem across thousands of unconscious specialist circuits at once (SRC1).

## 2. World before (era, place, why it mattered)

For decades after behaviorism, neuroscience cataloged specialized sensory modules without a testable model of what physically distinguishes an unconscious retinal signal from a conscious experience you can report.

At Wright Institute / Neurosciences Institute, San Diego, California in 1988, **Bernard J. Baars's contrastive analysis of conscious vs unconscious processing ('Global Workspace Theory')** turned an unresolved question about human mind and brain function into a controlled, reproducible empirical test (SRC1: Bernard J. Baars (2005). Global workspace theory of consciousness: toward a cognitive neuroscience of human experience. Progress in Brain Research).

## 3. People

- **Bernard J. Baars**: Cognitive neurobiologist who solved the paradox of why consciousness is tiny and serial while the unconscious brain is vast and parallel (SRC1).
- **Herbert Simon & Allen Newell**: AI pioneers whose 'blackboard architecture' (independent specialist modules sharing one central bulletin board) inspired Baars's neural workspace (SRC4).
- **Stanislas Dehaene & Jean-Pierre Changeux**: Neuroscientists who translated Baars's cognitive stage into the anatomical Global Neuronal Workspace of long-range frontoparietal pyramidal axons (SRC1).

Bernard J. Baars (b. 1946) drew inspiration from Allen Newell and Herbert Simon's speech-recognition 'blackboard' AI systems to formulate Global Workspace Theory (1988, 2005), later formalized anatomically by Stanislas Dehaene and Jean-Pierre Changeux.

Baars applied 'contrastive analysis' across dozens of matched unconscious-vs-conscious paradigms — binocular rivalry (two eyes, one conscious scene), backward visual masking, dichotic listening, automatic habits vs sudden novelty, and tip-of-the-tongue recall (SRC1, SRC2).

## 5. Results (numbers as reported)

Unconscious specialist circuits are huge, fast, and parallel, yet modularly isolated. Crossing the threshold into consciousness sacrifices parallel capacity (narrowing to ~1 coherent coalition at a time) in exchange for GLOBAL BROADCASTING across long-range cortico-cortical and thalamocortical hubs — letting vision, memory, language, and planning coordinate on novel problems (SRC1, SRC3).

- First formulated in his 1988 book A Cognitive Theory of Consciousness and synthesized neurobiologically in Progress in Brain Research (2005), Bernard Baars tackled a central riddle: why can an 86-billion-neuron supercomputer consciously hold ONLY ONE conversation or ambiguous image at a time? (SRC1)
- Baars used 'contrastive analysis' — comparing tight experimental pairs where nearly identical inputs stay unconscious vs cross into consciousness (e.g., binocular rivalry, masked words, unattended dichotic speech, automatic vs novel skills). (SRC1)
- Unconscious brain networks (grammar parsing, balance, retinal edge detection, heart regulation) are VAST, fast, parallel, and isolated — each specialist module does its job quickly without knowing what distant modules are doing. (SRC1)
- Conscious awareness, by contrast, is NARROW (~1 scene, 1 inner voice), serial, and slightly slower — yet buys one superpower no isolated module possesses: GLOBAL BROADCASTING. (SRC1)
- Like an actor stepping into a bright spot on a theater stage in front of a dark auditorium of thousands of silent experts, the instant ONE piece of information wins entry to the Global Workspace, it broadcasts simultaneously to memory, language, motor planning, and emotion. (SRC1)
- Consider binocular rivalry: show a house to the left eye and a face to the right eye simultaneously — both retinas and primary visual cortices fire continuously, yet you consciously see ONLY the face OR ONLY the house at any moment, broadcasting whichever wins across frontoparietal cortex. (SRC2)
- Crucially, Baars warned against the 'Cartesian Theater' fallacy (Dennett): there is NO tiny little person sitting inside your head watching the stage — the 'audience' IS the distributed network of unconscious cortical/subcortical specialist circuits receiving the broadcast. (SRC3)
- Recent multi-lab adversarial tests (Cogitate Consortium 2023-2025) put Global Workspace Theory head-to-head against Integrated Information Theory, confirming widespread cortical broadcast on task-relevant ignition while actively debating exact prefrontal vs posterior timing. (SRC4)

Several quantitative contrasts stood out across the published data. Your unconscious brain processes millions of sensory bits in parallel every second — yet your conscious mind can't even listen to two simple sentences at the exact same moment. Why? Because consciousness isn't the worker — it's the PA loudspeaker — a striking result given that only one voice can use a building-wide loudspeaker at a time without turning into static (SRC1). Feed a picture of a house into your left eye and a face into your right eye at the exact same instant: both eyes keep firing nonstop, yet your conscious world flips cleanly from 100% Face to 100% House every few seconds — notable because one winner takes the whole global broadcast stage (SRC2). Ever had a name stuck 'on the tip of your tongue'? Your conscious stage is empty — just broadcasting a silent 'WANTED' poster out to dark auditorium rows until five minutes later an unconscious memory specialist shouts 'Archimedes!' onto the stage — revealing how global broadcast lets you recruit specialist modules you don't even know how to search (SRC2). There is NO 'little viewer' sitting in the balcony of Baars's theater — the audience IS your motor, language, and memory networks listening in — highlighting that that single insight avoids the infinite homunculus trap (SRC3).

## 6. Twist, controversy, misconceptions

Philosophers like Daniel Dennett warned against imagining a tiny 'viewer' sitting inside a theater watching a screen — and Baars agreed 100%: there is NO inner homunculus. Global availability TO distributed specialist modules IS conscious access (SRC3). Today, multi-lab 'adversarial collaborations' (Cogitate Consortium / Ferrante et al. 2025, SRC4) pit Global Workspace predictions directly against rival theories in preregistered brain scans.

- **"Consciousness does all the heavy computational lifting in your brain while the unconscious is primitive."** — Unconscious specialist circuits handle stunningly complex syntax, vision, and motor math; consciousness provides flexible cross-brain integration when routines hit novelty. (SRC1)
- **"Global Workspace Theory claims there is a literal 'screen' watched by a mini-self in the frontal lobe."** — Baars explicitly modeled AI 'blackboard architectures': global availability TO specialist modules IS conscious access, with zero inner spectator. (SRC3)
- **"You can consciously multitask two verbal streams simultaneously with zero loss."** — Because the Global Workspace is serial (~one broadcast coalition at a time), 'multitasking' two conversations is actually rapid, costly stage-switching. (SRC1)
- **"Science has 100% settled every neural detail of consciousness."** — Global Workspace remains a leading testable contender actively refined through adversarial trials against posterior-cortex and integrated-information rivals. (SRC4)

## 7. What happened after (follow-ups, modern science)

Global Workspace Theory became the leading cognitive architecture bridging human consciousness research, clinical coma/anesthesia diagnostics, and modern AI attention architectures.

Subsequent peer-reviewed studies tested, refined, and bounded these findings. **SRC4** (Oscar Ferrante, Urszula Górska, Simon E. Henin, Rony Hirschhorn, Aya F. Khalaf et al. (2025). Further peer-reviewed replication, critique, and synthesis appeared across Bernard J. Baars, Stan Franklin, Thomas Zoega Ramsoy (2013). A Neural Global Workspace Model for Conscious Attention. Neural Networks (**SRC3**); and Bernard J. Baars, Stan Franklin (2009). CONSCIOUSNESS IS COMPUTATIONAL: THE LIDA MODEL OF GLOBAL WORKSPACE THEORY. International Journal of Machine Consciousness (**SRC5**).

At the neural-circuit level, **primary visual cortex** (local sensory specialist) contributes by processing raw features locally in parallel even before a stimulus wins global awareness, which shows both binocular-rivalry images active locally in early visual cortex while only one reaches awareness; meanwhile, the **prefrontal cortex** (frontoparietal broadcast hub) contributes by igniting long-range white-matter communication with parietal, temporal, and language regions once one coalition wins the stage, which visualizes Baars & Dehaene's global broadcast linking distant cortical specialists; meanwhile, **parietal cortex** (workspace partner hub) contributes co-maintaining the single winning conscious scene across the dorsal-frontoparietal bridge, which anchors the wide cortical broadcast network.

## 8. What it means for you

Bernard Baars explained why you can only think one conscious thought at a time inside an 86-billion-neuron brain: consciousness acts like a central PA microphone that broadcasts ONE winning problem across thousands of unconscious specialist circuits at once. Stop forcing your narrow conscious stage to juggle three verbal tasks at once — state ONE clear problem clearly onto the stage, then give your vast unconscious auditorium quiet space to return the answer.

Treat your conscious mind like a priceless one-speaker stage: stop jamming three half-read tabs onto it at once, broadcast one clear priority clearly, and give your quiet specialist modules room to work.

Core scientific concepts from this research translate into everyday terms as follows:

- **Global Workspace**: high-capacity long-range broadcast state where one winning representation becomes simultaneously available to language, memory, and action systems — like putting an urgent bulletin on the front page every department reads over breakfast.
- **unconscious specialist modules**: fast, parallel, isolated neural networks handling vision edges, grammar, posture, or stored names behind the scenes — like specialized engineers working quietly in separate soundproof labs.
- **binocular rivalry**: showing two different images to the two eyes simultaneously so conscious awareness alternates winner-take-all between them — like two radio stations on one frequency where one snaps into crystal clarity at a time.
- **contrastive analysis**: comparing matched unconscious vs conscious brain states to isolate what changes when awareness ignites — like weighing a suitcase full vs empty to find the weight of the contents.

In daily life, this mechanism surfaces in familiar situations:

- Forgetting a movie actor's name at dinner, broadcasting the mental question once, talking about dessert, and having the name pop onto your conscious stage ten minutes later.
- Learning to drive stick-shift (hogging 100% of your conscious stage) versus driving years later on unconscious specialist autopilot until sudden black ice pulls driving straight back onto the global stage.
- Trying to read a text while listening to a podcast — and realizing one stream went completely blank the instant the other took the microphone.
- Writing a tricky problem clearly on a whiteboard ('external workspace') before sleep so unconscious specialists work on it.
