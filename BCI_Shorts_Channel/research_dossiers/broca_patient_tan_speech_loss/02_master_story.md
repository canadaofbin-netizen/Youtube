# Paul Broca (1861) & Nina Dronkers (2007): Patient 'Tan', the Preserved Paris Brain & the Birth of Cortical Localization — Master Story (longform depth)

## 1. Cold open (the iconic moment)

For twenty-one years inside Paris's Bicêtre Hospital, everyone called 51-year-old Louis Victor Leborgne **'Monsieur Tan'** — because to almost every question on Earth, his mouth could utter only two syllables: *'Tan, tan!'* Yet when 36-year-old surgeon **Paul Broca** sat beside his bed on April 12, 1861, pulled out a pocket watch, and asked how many years he had been there, Leborgne's eyes gleamed with sharp intelligence as his left hand crisply flashed **five fingers four times, plus one: 21 years** (SRC1). Six days later, one un-sliced brain changed medicine forever — and 146 years after that, a modern MRI scanner uncovered the rest of Tan's secret (SRC_DRONKERS).

Recorded in April 1861, Bicêtre Hospital ward, Paris, the defining visual record centers on an 1861 Paris hospital room where a seated patient answers a frock-coated physician's pocket-watch question by crisply flashing finger counts ('21') beside an anatomical left-frontal brain model (SRC1, SRC_STILL). In April 1861, Paul Broca showed that Louis Victor Leborgne ('Patient Tan') kept sharp comprehension and finger-counting while losing speech to a left inferior frontal lesion — founding cortical localization and left-hemisphere dominance, later deepened when Nina Dronkers MRI-scanned Tan's preserved brain in 2007 (SRC1).

## 2. World before (era, place, why it mattered)

After Franz Joseph Gall's skull-bump 'phrenology' was discredited, holistic physiologists led by Pierre Flourens insisted the cerebral hemispheres operated as one uniform, indivisible mass — and that left and right hemispheres were identical twins.

At Bicêtre Hospital & Société d'Anthropologie de Paris (and Musée Dupuytren, Paris) in 1861, **Paul Broca's April 1861 Bicêtre Hospital study of Louis Victor Leborgne ('Monsieur Tan') & Nina Dronkers's 2007 High-Resolution MRI Re-Examination** turned an unresolved question about human mind and brain function into a controlled, reproducible empirical test (SRC1: Paul Broca (2011). Remarks on the Seat of Spoken Language, Followed by a Case of Aphasia (1861). Neuropsychology Review. [English translation of Paul Broca's April 18, 1861 Bulletins de la Société Anatomique de Paris report on Louis Victor Leborgne ('Tan')]).

## 3. People

- **Pierre Paul Broca**: 36-year-old Paris surgeon-anthropologist who examined Louis Victor Leborgne in April 1861 and wisely preserved Leborgne's intact un-sliced brain in alcohol so future centuries could re-scan it (SRC1).
- **Louis Victor Leborgne ('Patient Tan')**: 51-year-old former artisan frame-maker who lost speech at age 30 — retaining sharp comprehension, expressive facial prosody, and finger-counting while able to pronounce only the single syllable 'Tan' (SRC1).
- **Nina F. Dronkers**: UC Davis cognitive neuroscientist who placed Leborgne's and Lelong's preserved 1861 museum brains into a modern high-resolution MRI scanner in 2007 — discovering the lesion ran far deeper along the Superior Longitudinal / Arcuate Fasciculus (SRC_DRONKERS).

Paul Broca (1824-1880) examined both Leborgne ('Tan') and 84-year-old Lazare Lelong (who retained only five words) in 1861, preserving both brains intact in Paris until UC Davis neuroscientist Nina Dronkers scanned them in high-resolution MRI in 2007.

## 4. The event / experiment, step by step

Broca (1861; 2011 translation, SRC1) documented Leborgne's intact comprehension, facial expression, tongue mobility, arithmetic finger-counting, and 21-year medical timeline before correlating his focal cortical softening at autopsy — followed in 2007 by Dronkers et al. (SRC2) scanning the preserved brains at 1.5T/3T MRI without a single scalpel cut.

1. **April 1861 Bicêtre Bedside: Leborgne Answering '21 Years' With Crisp Left-Hand Finger Counts While Saying Only 'Tan, Tan'** (*April 1861, Bicêtre Hospital ward, Paris*). The scene unfolds inside a sunlit 1861 Paris hospital examination room with tall arched French windows, a crisp white-linened bedside chair, Paul Broca's silver pocket watch and leather clinical notebook on an oak side table, and an anatomical plaster model highlighting the left third frontal convolution. Paul Broca (wearing 1861 dark French physician frock coat, white cravat, and waistcoat) is holding open his silver pocket watch and asking Leborgne how many years he has lived at Bicêtre; Louis Victor Leborgne / 'Monsieur Tan' (wearing neat 1861 white linen hospital shirt and cardigan) is nodding with bright, alert eyes, gesturing 'Tan, tan' vocally while crisply opening his left hand four consecutive times (20) plus 1 single finger (= 21 years!). The experimental setup features **1861 silver pocket watch & '4 × 5 + 1 = 21 Years' ledger note** (proves Leborgne's sharp arithmetic, time reading, and spoken-question comprehension; refutes any confusion between speech loss and dementia), and **anatomical cerebral cast marking the Left 3rd Frontal Convolution** (pinpoints the exact asymmetric left-frontal locus presented on April 18, 1861; marks the birth of cortical localization). Paul Broca asks Leborgne how many years he has spent at Bicêtre and shows his pocket watch, and then Leborgne replies 'Tan, tan' with expressive pitch while flashing four crisp 5-finger counts plus 1 finger to answer '21' accurately. In response, Paul Broca records intact intelligence + preserved tongue motility alongside selective loss of *articulate language*; Leborgne expresses clear relief at being understood accurately. His mouth could pronounce only one syllable — 'Tan' — yet five seconds with Paul Broca's pocket watch proved a sharp, fully awake mind lived inside (SRC1, SRC2, SRC_M1, SRC_M2, SRC_STILL).

2. **2007 Paris MRI: Scanning Leborgne's 146-Year-Old Preserved Brain** (*2007, Paris / UC Davis neuroimaging suite (Dronkers et al.)*). Set within a clean bright modern MRI control suite displaying crisp 3D slices of Leborgne's preserved 1861 specimen. Nina Dronkers (wearing modern blazer) is pointing on the high-resolution monitor to the deep white-matter tract lesion (Superior Longitudinal Fasciculus) extending far deeper than surface Broca's area. Central to the procedure is **high-res 3D MRI slice display of Leborgne's 1861 brain** (reveals deep insula + SLF/arcuate tract involvement without cutting the historic specimen; Dronkers et al. 2007 Brain landmark). Dronkers scrolls from the famous 1861 surface dent deeper into the white-matter speech highway. Immediately afterward, French & US colleagues nod seeing a 146-year clinical mystery resolved. Because Paul Broca refused to slice 'Tan's' brain in 1861, a 2007 MRI scanner revealed what hid underneath for 146 years (SRC_DRONKERS, SRC_M1, SRC_M2).

3. **'We Speak With the Left Hemisphere!' (1865 Asymmetry Breakthrough)** (*1865, Société d'Anthropologie de Paris lecture hall*). The setup centers on a sunlit 19th-century oak lecture table displaying eight left-hemisphere case diagrams vs zero right-hemisphere aphasia cases. Paul Broca is presenting the 8-to-0 left-vs-right hemisphere chart to astonished Parisian anatomists. Central to the procedure is **8 Left vs 0 Right hemisphere case ledger** (overturns centuries of assuming symmetrical twin hemispheres; Broca 1865 landmark). Broca points across eight consecutive left-frontal lesion diagrams. Across the room, the anatomists realize human brain hemispheres specialize asymmetrically. Eight speech-loss brains in a row, all damaged on the LEFT — shattering the myth that our two hemispheres are identical twins (SRC1, SRC2).

4. **Singing Across the Detour: Melodic Intonation Rehabilitation** (*Modern speech-language therapy studio*). The setup centers on a warm sunlit therapy table where a therapist taps a gentle two-note rhythm with a left-hemisphere stroke survivor. The speech therapist & Survivor are tapping left-hand rhythm and singing 'Good mor-ning!' smoothly together using intact right-hemisphere melody circuits when plain speech stalls. Central to the procedure is **two-note melodic rhythm cards** (recruits intact right-hemisphere frontotemporal singing network; clinical payoff of Broca's lateralization discovery). The survivor struggles speaking prose, and then taps rhythm and sings the full three-word phrase clearly. At that moment, both beam at the unlocked voice. When the left hemisphere's speech bridge is damaged, tapping a melody can route real words across the intact right hemisphere (SRC1, SRC2).

## 5. Results (numbers as reported)

Broca localized articulate speech production to the **posterior third of the LEFT inferior frontal gyrus** (now Broca's area, BA 44/45) and by 1865 established left-hemisphere language dominance (*'Nous parlons avec l'hémisphère gauche!'*, SRC3).

- In early April 1861 in Paris, fierce scientific debate raged at the *Société d'Anthropologie*: holistic orthodoxy (Pierre Flourens) insisted the cerebral cortex was an undifferentiated whole after phrenology's collapse, while Ernest Auburtin vowed he would renounce localization forever if a patient lost speech WITHOUT frontal-lobe damage. (SRC1)
- Just days later (April 12, 1861), 51-year-old **Louis Victor Leborgne** arrived on surgeon **Paul Broca's** ward at Bicêtre Hospital — nicknamed **'Monsieur Tan'** across the hospital because for 21 years his sole spoken output to almost every question was **'Tan, tan!'** (plus one frustrated French oath when angry!). (SRC1)
- Broca tested Leborgne carefully at the bedside: Leborgne's tongue and lips were NOT paralyzed (he chewed, swallowed, and varied vocal pitch expressively!), and his intelligence and comprehension were remarkably sharp — ask how many years he had been at Bicêtre and he calmly opened his left hand **four times plus one finger = 21 years**; ask the time on Broca's pocket watch and he held up the exact fingers. (SRC1)
- Six days later Leborgne died of gangrene; on **April 18, 1861**, Broca presented Leborgne's brain in Paris showing a prominent egg-sized softening centered on the **posterior third of the LEFT inferior frontal gyrus** — and six months later **Lazare Lelong** (an 84-year-old laborer able to say only *oui, non, tois, toujours, Lelo*) showed a smaller lesion on the **exact same left frontal convolution**. (SRC1)
- By 1865, after seeing eight consecutive left-sided aphasia cases with zero right-sided equivalents, Broca proclaimed neurology's revolutionary asymmetric law: ***'Nous parlons avec l'hémisphère gauche!'*** ('We speak with the left hemisphere!'). (SRC3)
- Fortunately for science, Broca refused to slice Leborgne's and Lelong's brains open, preserving them whole in jars at Paris's Musée Dupuytren for 146 years. (SRC_DRONKERS)
- In **2007 (*Brain*)**, Nina Dronkers and French colleagues slid both 1861 brains into a high-resolution MRI scanner and uncovered a thrilling modern twist: surface 'Broca's area' (BA 44/45) was only the tip of the iceberg — Leborgne's and Lelong's lesions plunged deep into the **insula, basal ganglia, and the Superior Longitudinal / Arcuate Fasciculus** white-matter superhighway. (SRC_DRONKERS)
- That 2007 MRI solved a 20th-century clinical puzzle (Mohr 1978, SRC4): tiny strokes confined strictly to surface Broca's cortex cause weeks of apraxia that mostly recover, whereas permanent 'Tan'-level nonfluent aphasia requires severing the deep frontal-white-matter speech production network. (SRC_DRONKERS)
- By 1865, Broca's series of 8 consecutive left-hemisphere aphasia cases versus 0 right-hemisphere cases overturned cerebral symmetry dogma and set the stage for Carl Wernicke's 1874 sensory-aphasia discovery. (SRC3)

Several quantitative contrasts stood out across the published data. Ask 'Monsieur Tan' how long he has lived in Bicêtre Hospital: his mouth can only say **'Tan, tan!'**, yet his hand crisply flashes **5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 1 = 21 years** without a second's hesitation — proving thought and comprehension survived when spoken words vanished — a striking result given that losing speech production does NOT mean losing a sharp human mind (SRC1). Because Paul Broca wisely refused to slice Leborgne's brain up in 1861, **Nina Dronkers was able to put the actual 146-year-old brain of Patient Tan into a modern MRI scanner in 2007** — notable because a 19th-century clinical decision unlocked a 21st-century neuroimaging breakthrough (SRC_DRONKERS). Dronkers's 2007 MRI revealed Leborgne's famous surface dent hid deep destruction of the **Superior Longitudinal / Arcuate Fasciculus** — solving why small surface Broca strokes recover while 'Tan' never spoke a sentence for 21 years — revealing how Broca's patient had a bigger lesion than 'Broca's Area' itself (SRC_DRONKERS). Before Broca's 1865 report, anatomists assumed the left and right halves of the human brain were mirror-image twins like two lungs or kidneys — *'We speak with the left hemisphere'* shattered brain symmetry forever — marking the discovery of cerebral lateralization (SRC3).

## 6. Twist, controversy, misconceptions

In the late 20th century, neurologists like J. P. Mohr (1978, SRC4) noticed something puzzling: small strokes hitting ONLY surface Broca's area caused temporary speech stumbling rather than permanent 'Tan'-style mutism. Why had Leborgne stayed speechless for 21 years? When Nina Dronkers slid Tan's actual 1861 brain into an MRI tube in 2007 (SRC_DRONKERS), the hidden interior lit up: Leborgne's lesion plunged deep beneath surface Broca's area through the **insula and the Superior Longitudinal / Arcuate Fasciculus** white-matter highway. Broca's historic restraint in keeping the brain whole allowed 21st-century network neuroscience to complete his 1861 case.

- **"Grammar and speech live inside one isolated postage-stamp box ('Broca's Area') that works all by itself."** — Modern MRI — including Dronkers's 2007 scan of Tan's own brain! — proves fluent speech requires a coordinated network spanning inferior frontal cortex, insula, premotor cortex, and deep arcuate/SLF white-matter tracts. (SRC_DRONKERS)
- **"Patients with Broca's nonfluent aphasia cannot understand what you say or think clearly."** — Everyday comprehension and intelligence remain largely intact (save complex reversible passive grammar), which is why shouting slowly as if to a child is deeply insulting to an aphasic adult. (SRC1)
- **"Broca jumped straight from phrenology skull bumps to brain maps."** — Broca explicitly rejected skull-bump phrenology; he paired careful living bedside cognitive exams with post-mortem cortical pathology. (SRC1)

## 7. What happened after (follow-ups, modern science)

Broca's 1861 presentation founded modern behavioral neurology, functional brain mapping, awake neurosurgical speech mapping, and Melodic Intonation Therapy (using intact right-hemisphere singing circuits to help aphasic survivors speak again).

Subsequent peer-reviewed studies tested, refined, and bounded these findings. **SRC2** (Evelina Fedorenko, Idan A. Blank (2020). Broca’s Area Is Not a Natural Kind. Trends in Cognitive Sciences) provided a functional neuroimaging reassessment dissociating language-selective vs domain-general subregions inside Broca's area. **SRC4** (J. P. Mohr, M. S. Pessin, S. Finkelstein, H. H. Funkenstein et al. (1978). Broca aphasia. Neurology) contributed a landmark clinicopathological study demonstrating that lesions restricted solely to surface Broca's area cause transient mutism rather than permanent Broca's aphasia. **SRC_DRONKERS** (N. F. Dronkers, O. Plaisant, M. T. Iba-Zizen, E. A. Cabanis (2007). Paul Broca's historic cases: high resolution MR imaging of the brains of Leborgne and Lelong. Brain) presented a high-resolution 3D MRI re-examination of Leborgne's and Lelong's preserved 1861 brains revealing deep superior longitudinal / arcuate fasciculus involvement. Further peer-reviewed replication, critique, and synthesis appeared across Francis Schiller (1983). Paul Broca and the history of aphasia. Neurology (**SRC3**); David A. Lee (1983). Paul Broca and the history of aphasia. Neurology (**SRC5**); and Robert J. Joynt (1964). Paul Pierre Broca: His Contribution to the Knowledge of Aphasia. Cortex (**SRC6**).

At the neural-circuit level, **Wernicke's area** (intact auditory comprehension) contributes by decoding Paul Broca's spoken bedside questions cleanly so Leborgne knows his exact 21-year stay and pocket-watch time, which shows posterior comprehension cortex remained functional; meanwhile, **Broca's area** (left inferior frontal speech-assembly lesion) contributes by blocking spoken syllable sequencing on the posterior third of the left third frontal convolution so output collapses to 'Tan, tan', which captures Broca's historic April 1861 anatomical localization; meanwhile, **white matter** (deep arcuate / SLF tract disconnection (2007 MRI)) contributes by extending beneath surface cortex through deep perisylvian white matter as revealed when Dronkers scanned Leborgne's preserved 1861 brain, which integrates Dronkers et al. (2007 Brain) network correction.

## 8. What it means for you

In April 1861, Paul Broca showed that Louis Victor Leborgne ('Patient Tan') kept sharp comprehension and finger-counting while losing speech to a left inferior frontal lesion — founding cortical localization and left-hemisphere dominance, later deepened when Nina Dronkers MRI-scanned Tan's preserved brain in 2007. When communicating with someone recovering from expressive aphasia after a stroke, speak with normal adult respect, give unhurried time, and welcome gestures, writing, or singing (which recruits intact right-hemisphere melody circuits!).

Never mistake broken speech output for broken intelligence — behind halting words after a stroke sits an adult mind listening, counting, and understanding every nuance.

Core scientific concepts from this research translate into everyday terms as follows:

- **Broca's Area**: posterior left inferior frontal gyrus (Brodmann areas 44/45) coordinating speech motor sequencing and syntactic assembly — comparable to the vocal conductor organizing thoughts into spoken syllables.
- **expressive (Broca's) aphasia**: loss of fluent spoken output despite intact vocal muscles and preserved everyday comprehension — comparable to knowing every word you want to say while the bridge to your mouth is out.
- **cerebral lateralization**: Broca's 1865 discovery that speech production is strongly left-hemisphere dominant in ~95% of right-handed adults — comparable to two brain hemispheres with specialized day jobs.
- **Superior Longitudinal / Arcuate Fasciculus**: deep white-matter fiber highway revealed in Dronkers's 2007 MRI of Tan's brain to be essential for lasting speech loss — comparable to the underground trunk cable beneath Broca's surface station.
- **Melodic Intonation rescue**: clinical phenomenon where patients unable to speak 'Happy Birthday' or 'Thank you' in prose can often SING the words via intact right-hemisphere music circuits — comparable to singing across a detour when the speech bridge is closed.

In daily life, this mechanism surfaces in familiar situations:

- Having a word sit 100% clear in your thoughts ('tip-of-the-tongue') while your mouth temporarily refuses to assemble the syllables.
- Remembering never to equate slow or halted speech after a stroke with dimmed intelligence.
- Marveling that a preserved 1861 brain sat waiting 146 years in a Paris jar until MRI technology was invented to peer inside it.
