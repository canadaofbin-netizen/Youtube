# Scangos, Chang & Krystal (2021): Closed-Loop Amygdala-Guided Brain Pacemaker for Severe Depression

## Act I — The Paradoxical Setup (UCSF Weill Institute for Neurosciences, University of California, San Francisco, 2021)
Katherine Scangos, Edward Chang, Andrew Krystal, and colleagues (Nature Medicine, Oct 2021) mapped a 36-year-old woman ('Sarah') with severe treatment-resistant depression using 10 days of multi-site intracranial stereo-EEG, discovering a right-amygdala gamma biomarker that predicted symptom spikes with 87% accuracy and triggered a closed-loop ventral striatum pacemaker (~300 six-second pulses/day) that dropped her MADRS depression score from 36 to 10! For decades, textbook assumptions treated this phenomenon as a simple psychological quirk rather than a precise, millisecond-level neural circuit calculation. When the research team led by Katherine W. Scangos, Edward F. Chang & Andrew D. Krystal brought participants into the laboratory at UCSF Weill Institute for Neurosciences, University of California, San Francisco, they designed a rigorous quantitative protocol to isolate the exact physiological variables driving human perception and behavior.

## Act II — Quantitative Experimental Protocol & Behavioral Results
### Empirical Finding 1
In Scangos, Khambhati, Chang & Krystal (2021 Nature Medicine), 10 days of continuous 10-lead intracranial stereo-EEG mapping in a 36-year-old woman with severe treatment-resistant depression identified bilateral amygdala high-frequency gamma power (30–110 Hz) as a personalized neural biomarker that classified high vs low symptom severity with 87 percent cross-validated accuracy. (Source: SRC1).

### Empirical Finding 2
Delivering brief 6-second bursts of 100 Hz, 1 mA electrical stimulation to the right ventral capsule/ventral striatum (VC/VS) exclusively when right amygdala gamma power crossed threshold suppressed pathologically elevated amygdala gamma activity and acutely relieved depressive distress without conscious paresthesia. (Source: SRC1).

### Empirical Finding 3
The implanted NeuroPace RNS closed-loop device delivered approximately 300 six-second stimulation bursts per day (totalling roughly 30 minutes of daily stimulation, capped to stop before 8:00 p.m. to protect sleep) rather than 24-hour continuous open-loop stimulation. (Source: SRC1).

### Empirical Finding 4
The patient's Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) score dropped from 36 out of 54 at pre-surgical baseline to 14 within 12 days of closed-loop activation and achieved clinical remission at 10 (a 72 percent reduction) sustained across 15 months of follow-up. (Source: SRC1).

### Empirical Finding 5
Daily visual-analog depression severity ratings (VAS-D) fell from 77 out of 100 before closed-loop therapy to 16 out of 100 after activation, with daily detections correlating strongly (r = 0.68) with naturalistic emotional stress. (Source: SRC1).

### Empirical Finding 6
In Scangos et al. (Jan 2021 Nature Medicine), identical intracranial stimulation parameters produced opposite mood effects depending on whether the patient's baseline brain network was in a low-arousal vs hyper-aroused state, explaining why fixed continuous DBS trials like Holtzheimer et al. (2017 Lancet Psychiatry) struggled. (Source: SRC2).

### Empirical Finding 7
Effective white-matter tractography and evoked potential mapping proved that right amygdala and right VC/VS formed a tightly coupled structural-functional subnetwork with a 25–40 ms directional signal latency. (Source: SRC1).

### Empirical Finding 8
Alagapan et al. (2023 Nature) further demonstrated in a 10-patient cohort that intracranial electrophysiological biomarkers can predict clinical relapse weeks before behavioral depression scores worsen. (Source: SRC5).

## Act III — Inside the Contained Living 4-Color 3D Brain
In the **amygdala** (right amygdala 30–110 Hz gamma-burst depression alarm sensor), neuroimaging and electrophysiological recordings demonstrate detecting impending depressive symptom spikes with 87% accuracy and triggering the closed-loop pacemaker (Scangos et al. 2021 Nature Medicine (SRC1).). This circuit interaction explains why conscious willpower or verbal reassurance alone cannot override the subcortical timing gate.

In the **ventral striatum** (ventral capsule / ventral striatum (VC/VS) 6-second stimulation target), neuroimaging and electrophysiological recordings demonstrate firing a 6-second 100 Hz pulse (~300 times/day) that quiets amygdala gamma hyperactivity and restores reward motivation (Closed-loop stimulation lead in Scangos et al. 2021.). This circuit interaction explains why conscious willpower or verbal reassurance alone cannot override the subcortical timing gate.

In the **anterior cingulate cortex** (subgenual and dorsal cingulate mood network normalization), neuroimaging and electrophysiological recordings demonstrate shifting the broader limbic-prefrontal network from rigid depressive rumination into sustained remission (MADRS 36 -> 10) (Mayberg 2005 & Alagapan 2023 Nature.). This circuit interaction explains why conscious willpower or verbal reassurance alone cannot override the subcortical timing gate.
