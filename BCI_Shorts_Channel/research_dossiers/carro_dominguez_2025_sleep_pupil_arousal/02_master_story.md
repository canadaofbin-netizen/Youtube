# Why Your Pupils Pulse All Night Even When Your Eyes Are Shut

## 1. The Everyday Puzzle: Unexplained Grogginess After Eight Hours in Bed
Millions of adults wake up exhausted even after spending seven or eight hours inside a dark, quiet bedroom with zero conscious memory of waking during the night. Common consumer sleep advice reduces rest to rough movement-based wrist stages and assumes that once eyelids close, the central arousal system settles into a flat, low-power pause until morning. Yet an everyday contradiction persists: why can a quiet floorboard creak or distant hallway sound jolt a sleeper wide awake at 2:15 AM, while an identical noise twenty minutes later leaves them deeply undisturbed? In February 2025, researchers at ETH Zürich published direct overnight infrared video in *Nature Communications* (`SRC1`, DOI `10.1038/s41467-025-57289-5`) that resolves this paradox visually and quantitatively. Even in complete darkness behind closed eyes, the human brain's noradrenergic arousal circuitry never stops pulsing—driving rhythmic ~60-second waves and sudden spikes in pupil width that open and close our sensory gate all night long.

## 2. How ETH Zürich Filmed Sleeping Pupils Inside Total Darkness (`SRC1`)
Manuel Carro-Domínguez, Stephanie Huwiler, Stella Oberlin, Timona Leandra Oesch, Gabriela Badii, Anita Lüthi, Nicole Wenderoth, Sarah Nadine Meissner, and Caroline Lustenberger conducted the experiment inside the Neural Control of Movement Lab at ETH Zürich under ethics protocol BASEC2022-00340. Eighteen healthy adults were recruited; one volunteer was excluded prior to overnight monitoring because an OSDI ocular-surface dryness score above 12 risked discomfort, leaving 17 included healthy adults (10 female, 7 male; mean age `29.83 ± 8.69` years; body mass index `17–30 kg/m^2`; paid CHF 70 for participation). Each participant entered a pitch-dark acoustic sleep suite for a 7.5-hour monitored night wearing high-density polysomnography (`Fpz`, `Cz`, `O1`, `O2`, `M1` EEG, bilateral EOG, submental EMG, and ECG). To overcome the fundamental obstacle that eyelids block cameras in human sleep, clinicians applied vitamin A ointment to protect the right ocular surface, taped the right upper eyelid gently slightly open inside its natural palpebral fissure, sealed a clear perforated PRO Optha S transparent eye bandage over the orbit, and fitted a portable dark-room infrared eye-tracking monocular camera for the first 4 hours of the night. Because invisible near-infrared illumination produces zero visible glare in a dark room, pure pupillary light reflexes were eliminated. Fifteen of the 17 included sleepers cleared the strict quality bar of `>= 44.61%` valid unoccluded eye-tracker frames (`Supplementary Fig. 1B`), averaging `75.69 ± 5.66%` valid tracking across multi-hour NREM and REM sleep—and only two of 17 participants recalled briefly noticing the mild unfamiliar sensation upon morning awakening.

## 3. Macro Architecture Drops Into Minimal Pupil Size — Yet Fast Micro-Pulses Persist
On the macro scale across sleep stages (`Figure 1F`, linear mixed-effects model `F(4, 57.43) = 168.90, p < 0.001`), the pupil-to-iris ratio shifted sharply with global vigilance: largest during relaxed Wake, dropping progressively across transitions into light Stage N1 and Stage N2 sleep, and reaching equally constricted minima during both deep slow-wave Stage N3 sleep and REM dreaming sleep (`Supplementary Fig. 2`). Crucially, across every stage, continuous fluctuations in pupil diameter correlated positively with the prefrontal `Fpz` `30–45 Hz` aperiodic EEG spectral slope (repeated-measures correlation `R(4264) = 0.34, p < 0.001`, 95% CI `[0.31, 0.37]`, `Supplementary Fig. 3`), extending Lendner et al.'s (2020, `SRC2`) electrophysiological human arousal marker straight into autonomic pupillometry. Yet zoomed inside Stage N2 sleep—which represents nearly half of adult nightly rest—the pupil refused to hold still. Frequency decomposition exposed ~0.017 Hz infraslow fluctuations (~60-second period) locked cleanly out of phase with `Cz` `12–16 Hz` sigma sleep-spindle power (`F(3, 48.00) = 10.853, p < 0.001`, `Figure 2D`, `Supplementary Table 4`): pupil dilation lagged behind protective sigma-spindle surges by roughly 30% of each slow cycle (~108 degrees; lag cross-correlation `R = -0.26, p < 0.001`, `Figure 2G`). In plain terms, every minute of N2 sleep swings like a pendulum between calm, protected, spindle-rich quiescence and rising brainstem vigilance.

## 4. Three Distinct Brainwave Signatures Locked to Constriction and Dilation (`Figure 3`, `Table S7`)
Event-related locking between 50 Hz infrared pupil frames and concurrent scalp EEG exposed sharply opposing autonomic trajectories around the three cardinal waveforms of Stage N2 sleep (`Supplementary Table 7`, `Supplementary Fig. 6`):
- **Pre-Spindle Constriction (`-11.51 ± 1.46%`)**: Just prior to and during clustered `12–16 Hz` sleep spindle trains (`Figure 3C`), pupil diameter dropped sharply by `-11.51 ± 1.46%` below general N2 baseline (`p < 0.05`). Reduced brainstem noradrenergic firing releases thalamic reticular neurons to oscillate in spindle burst mode, physically gating sensory traffic out of the cortex (`Dang-Vu et al., 2010`, `SRC5`).
- **K-Complex Dilation (`+13.49 ± 2.21%`)**: Immediately after isolated cortical K-complex waveforms (`Figure 3A`), pupil diameter swelled by `+13.49 ± 2.21%` within `10` seconds post-onset (`p < 0.05`), accompanied by a transient heart-rate inflection that quickly subsided back toward rest—capturing a sub-awakening sentinel orienting response.
- **Spontaneous Sleep Arousal Surges (`+35.37 ± 9.88%`)**: When brief micro-arousals disrupted N2 EEG without waking conscious memory (`Figure 3B`), pupils surged outward by `+35.37 ± 9.88%` peaking roughly `7` seconds after arousal onset (`p < 0.05`), synchronized with a significant `+11.18 ± 2.31%` acceleration in instantaneous heart rate (`Supplementary Fig. 7`).

## 5. Why Soft 45 dB Sounds Only Boost Deep Sleep When Pupils Are Small (`Figures 4–5`, `Movie 1`)
To test whether pupil diameter directly governs how external bedroom noise hits a sleeping cortex, the ETH Zürich investigators played calibrated sequences of `10` soft `50 ms` pink-noise clicks spaced at `1 Hz` (`45 dB` volume—softer than a quiet conversation) versus silent sham windows during Stage N2 sleep (`n = 8` continuous pupillometry in the V1 auditory stimulation cohort; `n = 10` EEG; extending `Huwiler et al., 2022`, `SRC6`). Relative to sham windows, verum auditory tones drove robust pupil dilations during both early stimulation (`+13.77 ± 4.81%, p = 0.046`) and late stimulation (`+34.87 ± 12.90%, p = 0.046`), visually documented in `Supplementary Movie 1` (50 Hz infrared right-eye video at 4x playback speed synced directly below `Fpz`, `Cz`, and pupil traces). Most remarkably, splitting trials by the pre-tone baseline pupil revealed opposite plasticity (`Figure 5A–B`, `Supplementary Fig. 9–10`):
- When sleepers entered stimulation with a **small baseline pupil** (`-25.21 ± 3.40%` below general N2 average, `t(16.3) = -5.527`), gentle `45 dB` clicks triggered a statistically significant surge in protective `12–16 Hz` sigma sleep spindles (`+5.53 ± 2.04 µV²`, `p = 0.044`) alongside restorative `0.5–4 Hz` delta slow waves (`+928.67 ± 381.79 µV²`, `p = 0.031`).
- Conversely, when identical `45 dB` sounds arrived atop an already **large baseline pupil** (`+18.54 ± 5.50%` above general N2, `t(16.3) = 3.37, p = 0.004`), stimulation yielded near-zero spindle gain (`+0.08 ± 2.17 µV²`, `p = 0.97`) and zero delta increase (`-105.90 ± 277.54 µV²`; baseline state x condition interactions `F_sigma(1, 21) = 5.66, p = 0.027` and `F_delta(1, 28) = 6.77, p = 0.015`).

## 6. Circuit Link: Brainstem Locus Coeruleus, Thalamus, and Prefrontal Cortex (`SRC3`, `SRC4`, `SRC7`, `SRC8`)
Because dark goggles held optical luminance at zero lux throughout sleep, rod-cone constriction reflexes are completely ruled out. Converging animal single-unit neurophysiology (`Joshi et al., 2016`, `SRC4`), human concurrent 3T BOLD-fMRI pupillometry (`Murphy et al., 2014`, `SRC3`), and human pupillometric brainstem studies (`de Gee et al., 2017`, `SRC8`; synthesized by `Mather, 2023`, `SRC7`) converge on a compact deep-brain loop anchored by the **brainstem locus coeruleus**. Tonic and phasic bursts of norepinephrine rising from the dorsal pons simultaneously widen pupils via autonomic iris pathways and depolarize thalamocortical relay neurons in the **thalamus**, shifting the thalamus out of protective `12–16 Hz` spindle mode and heightening `30–45 Hz` arousal readiness across **prefrontal** and **auditory** cortices.

## 7. Honest Methodological Limits & Critiques
Rigorous science communication must pair exciting video proof with explicit boundaries. First, filming human pupils continuously in pitch darkness required gently taping one eyelid slightly open beneath vitamin A ointment and a protective shield for the initial 4 hours, yielding `15` continuous eye-tracked sleepers overall and `8` pupil-tracked sleepers in the V1 auditory stimulation cohort; home-friendly non-invasive confirmation across older cohorts and whole 8-hour nights remains ongoing. Second, non-luminance pupil dilation is an indirect peripheral readout influenced by convergent brainstem noradrenergic and cholinergic nuclei rather than an invasive microelectrode inside human locus coeruleus cells. Even within these stated bounds, `Supplementary Movie 1` proves that human sleep is not an inert switch—it is a minute-by-minute neurochemical tide deciding whether night sounds heal deep sleep or puncture it.
